It is very kind of you to reach out to your classmate. When someone shares news of a layoff, they are often feeling vulnerable, anxious, or even a sense of failure. 

Your original draft contains some very deep, intellectual observations about the economy and global politics. However, **when responding to a friend in distress, it is usually better to lead with empathy and personal connection** before moving into political or economic theories. If you focus too much on "why" the world is changing, she might feel that her personal struggle is being minimized.

Since you want to elaborate on your thoughts, I have prepared three different versions depending on the "vibe" of your friendship.

### Option 1: Warm, Empathetic & Philosophical (Best for a close friend)
*This version prioritizes her feelings first, then shares your perspective on the "new chapter" to provide mutual encouragement.*

"I was so sorry to hear your news. Please know I’m thinking of you. To be honest, I’ve been feeling the same tremors in my own career as a software developer—it feels like the world is shifting under our feet, whether due to AI or these broader economic changes.

It feels like we are witnessing a massive structural shift, perhaps even a 'k-shaped' reality that is impacting so many professionals right now. Some might see it as the end of an era, but I’m trying to view it through a different lens. I have truly enjoyed my life and career, and I’m trying to see this not as a loss, but as an invitation to open a brand new chapter. We’ve worked hard; maybe it’s time to see what else life has in store for us beyond the office."

### Option 2: Intellectual & Observational (Best if you always discuss politics/news)
*This version keeps your original thoughts about the USD and global power shifts but frames them as a way of "making sense" of the chaos together.*

"Thank you for sharing that with me. I am actually navigating some similar uncertainty in my software development role. It’s a strange time to be a professional; between the rise of AI and these k-shaped economic shifts, it feels like the old rules of the USA and the strong USD era are being rewritten. We might be watching a global pivot in real-time.

Even though the macro-environment feels volatile and unpredictable, I am choosing to stay positive. I’ve had a wonderful career, and I want to approach this transition with grace. Instead of fighting the tide, I’m preparing to embrace a new chapter of life. Let’s stay in touch as we both navigate these changes."

### Option 3: Short, Supportive & Strong (Best if you want to be brief)
*This is a "strength in numbers" approach. It tells her she isn't alone without getting too deep into theory.*

"I am so sorry to hear about the layoff. I wanted to let you know you aren't alone—I'm facing similar shifts in my software development career due to the changing economic and tech landscape. It feels like the world is undergoing a massive transformation right now.

However, I am trying to look forward with hope. I’ve enjoyed a great career, and I’m looking at this as an opportunity to start a fresh chapter of life. I’m rooting for you, and I’m here if you ever want to grab a coffee and talk (about work or anything else!)"

***

### A few tips for your final version:

*   **The "Bourgeoisie" Note:** In English, the word "bourgeoisie" is very heavy and has strong Marxist connotations. If your friend is not a political scholar, she might find it a bit jarring in a sympathy message. I replaced it in the suggestions with "professionals" or "the economic landscape" to keep the flow smooth.
*   **Validate her first:** Always start with "I am sorry to hear that" or "That sounds difficult." This ensures she feels heard before you move into the "big picture" talk.
*   **The "New Chapter" is your strongest point:** This is the most beautiful part of your message. It turns a "negative" (layoff) into a "positive" (new beginning). This will give her hope!
